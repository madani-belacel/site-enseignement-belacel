## Contenu détaillé
### What is Artificial Intelligence?
Artificial intelligence (AI) is a computer program that learns from data to recognise patterns, understand language or make decisions. For a primary-school child we can say: "it is a computer that learns by looking at lots of examples". "Artificial" means "made by humans", as opposed to the natural intelligence of living beings.
Three key words: <strong>data</strong> (the examples provided), <strong>algorithm</strong> (the recipe of calculations) and <strong>model</strong> (the result of learning, which we reuse). Without data, no AI: it is the raw material.
> 💡 **<strong>Class-ready definition:</strong> "AI is like a pupil who first learns by looking at lots of examples, and then becomes able to answer on their own."**

### A short history of AI (5 dates to know)
The idea is not new: from the 1950s, researchers dreamed of machines that calculate and think. Here are the key dates to remember when explaining AI to your future pupils.

| Year | Event | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1950 | Alan Turing publishes "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" | He asks: can a machine think? The imitation game. |
| 1956 | Dartmouth Conference (USA) | The name "artificial intelligence" is coined. |
| 1997 | Deep Blue beats chess world champion Kasparov | First machine victory over a human in a game. |
| 2012 | Deep learning breakthrough (deep neural networks) | Computers recognise images much better. |
| 2022 | Public launch of ChatGPT | Conversational AI becomes accessible to everyone, including school. |

### The three main families of AI
We can distinguish three levels of AI. Today, everything we use is narrow AI: it excels at one single specific task.

- <strong>Narrow AI</strong>: a single task — translation, image recognition, spam filtering… Excellent in its domain, useless elsewhere.
- <strong>General AI</strong>: would equal a human in all intellectual tasks. Does not exist yet; it is a research goal.
- <strong>Super-intelligence</strong>: would exceed human intelligence. A hypothetical scenario (science fiction for now, but it fuels ethical debates).
> 💡 **<strong>Trainer tip:</strong> use the toolbox image — "narrow AI tools are specialised tools, not a magic Swiss knife".**

### AI in everyday life (concrete examples)
Your students use AI without knowing it, several times a day. Ask them to give examples before showing this list.

- The <strong>GPS</strong> that picks the fastest route (traffic prediction).
- The <strong>spam filter</strong> of e-mail (message classification).
- <strong>Netflix / YouTube</strong>: "because you watched…" suggestions (recommendation engine).
- <strong>Facial recognition</strong> to unlock the phone.
- <strong>Voice assistants</strong> (Siri, Google Assistant) that understand speech.
- <strong>ChatGPT / Gemini / Grok</strong>: conversational and generative AI.
- <strong>Google Translate</strong> and automatic subtitles on YouTube.

### What AI can do… and cannot do

- <strong>✅ What it does well:</strong> summarise a text, translate, generate an image or an exercise, fix spelling, adapt a text to a reading level, produce a first draft of a worksheet.
- <strong>⚠️ What you must check:</strong> it can be wrong ("hallucination": inventing facts with confidence), reproduce biases present in its data, and mix languages.
- <strong>❌ What it does not do:</strong> it does not really understand meaning, has no intention, no emotions, and is not "responsible" for its answers.
> 💡 **<strong>Golden rule for the teacher:</strong> never place an AI tool between a pupil and their learning without checking, and keep your professional judgement. AI proposes, the teacher disposes.**

### Why this matters for the future primary teacher
Three main reasons, developed further in sessions 2 to 9 of this module:

- <strong>Save time:</strong> prepare a reading worksheet, a quiz or an adapted example in minutes instead of an hour.
- <strong>Differentiate:</strong> offer a simpler or richer version of the same exercise to each pupil.
- <strong>Educate in media literacy:</strong> your pupils will grow up with AI; they must learn what it is and how to use it critically.
> 💡 **<strong>Class example (shared in session 6):</strong> a maths problem restated "with dinosaurs" thanks to AI, to motivate a reluctant pupil.**

## À retenir
- AI is a program that learns from data (examples) to recognise, understand or decide.
- Today we only use narrow AI, specialised in a single task.
- AI does not "think" like a human: it computes and imitates, and it can get things wrong (hallucinations).
- For the teacher: AI is a planning assistant, never a replacement for professional judgement.
- Key dates: 1956 (term coined), 1997 (Deep Blue), 2012 (deep learning), 2022 (ChatGPT).

## Glossaire
- **Intelligence Artificielle (IA)** : A computer program able to learn from data to perform a task (recognise, understand, decide).
- **Données** : The examples (texts, images, numbers) given to AI so it can learn.
- **Algorithme** : A finite sequence of precise steps to solve a problem (a calculation recipe).
- **Modèle** : The result of an AI's learning, reused afterwards to make predictions.
- **Apprentissage automatique** : A method where AI learns rules from examples, instead of being given hand-written rules.
- **Hallucination** : A generative AI error when it confidently states a false or invented fact.
